# Group 15 - Code of Conduct

**Project Team:** Group 15  
**Methodology:** Scrum and Agile Development

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Code of Conduct establishes the rules, responsibilities, and professional standards that all Group 15 members must follow throughout the project.

Its purpose is to maintain a respectful, fair, responsible, secure, and collaborative working environment. All members are expected to follow these rules during project meetings, development activities, code reviews, GitHub collaboration, academic submissions, and other project-related activities.

This Code of Conduct applies equally to every team member, regardless of role or individual experience.

## 3. Respect, Equality, and Inclusion

All members must treat one another fairly and respectfully.

The following behaviors are not acceptable:

- Discrimination based on personal characteristics.
- Bullying, harassment, threats, or intimidation.
- Insults, personal attacks, or offensive language.
- Deliberately excluding members from relevant project information or discussions.
- Disrespecting another member's ideas without reasonable consideration.

Different opinions are welcome when expressed constructively. Criticism must focus on the work, decisions, or processes rather than attacking an individual.

## 4. Communication and Meeting Attendance

Members must communicate honestly, clearly, and professionally through the team's agreed communication channels.

Every member is expected to:

- Attend agreed Scrum meetings and project discussions on time.
- Provide accurate updates about completed work, ongoing tasks, and blockers.
- Inform the team in advance when unable to attend a meeting.
- Respond to important project communications within a reasonable, team-agreed timeframe.
- Share relevant information that could affect deadlines, quality, security, or other members' work.

Repeatedly ignoring important messages, providing misleading progress reports, or failing to communicate significant problems is unacceptable.

## 5. Task Ownership and Deadlines

Each member must take responsibility for assigned tasks and complete them according to the agreed acceptance criteria and deadlines.

Members must:

- Understand their tasks before beginning implementation.
- Update the relevant GitHub issue or project board to reflect actual progress.
- Raise questions or request assistance when requirements are unclear.
- Inform the Scrum Master and relevant team members as early as possible when a deadline may be missed.
- Provide a realistic revised completion estimate when delays occur.
- Ensure that unfinished work and known problems are clearly reported.

Members must not claim that a task is complete when required functionality, testing, documentation, or review remains outstanding.

Deadlines may be changed through team agreement when justified. Members must not change shared deadlines or task ownership without informing the relevant people.

## 6. Scrum Participation and Team Responsibilities

Group 15 will follow the agreed Scrum and Agile practices.

Members are expected to participate in:

- **Sprint Planning:** Agree on Sprint goals, priorities, responsibilities, and realistic commitments.
- **Daily Stand-ups:** Share progress, planned work, and blockers.
- **Sprint Reviews:** Demonstrate completed work and collect feedback.
- **Sprint Retrospectives:** Identify problems and agree on improvements.

The Product Owner is responsible for clarifying and prioritizing product requirements. The Scrum Master facilitates Scrum activities and supports the removal of blockers. Developers are responsible for implementing, testing, and maintaining assigned functionality.

These roles must work collaboratively. No role gives a member permission to disregard this Code of Conduct or make unreasonable demands of other members.

## 7. Collaboration and Fair Contribution

All members must contribute actively and fairly to the shared project objectives.

Members are expected to:

- Share useful knowledge, documentation, and technical findings.
- Assist teammates when reasonably possible.
- Avoid deliberately withholding information needed by other members.
- Respect individual contributions and acknowledge assistance received.
- Discuss workload imbalances openly and agree on reasonable adjustments.
- Avoid assigning blame for genuine mistakes without first understanding the circumstances.

No member should repeatedly leave agreed responsibilities to others without a reasonable explanation or prior agreement.

Contribution records must reflect actual work. Team members must not claim credit for work completed by others or deliberately misrepresent another member's contribution.

## 8. GitHub and Version Control Rules

All project code and related changes must follow the team's agreed GitHub workflow.

Members must:

- Work on appropriate feature branches.
- Use meaningful commit messages that describe the actual changes.
- Link relevant issues and pull requests where appropriate.
- Review changes and resolve merge conflicts carefully.
- Follow the agreed pull request approval and merging process.
- Keep shared branches, including `master` or `main` and `develop`, consistent with the team's branch protection and review rules.
- Pull and integrate relevant changes before continuing work when necessary.
- Report accidental deletions, overwritten work, or repository problems immediately.

Members must not:

- Push unreviewed changes directly to protected branches when approval is required.
- Delete or rewrite shared Git history without team authorization.
- Force-push to shared branches without explicit agreement.
- Merge changes that knowingly break the application or existing functionality.
- Commit passwords, API keys, access tokens, or other confidential information.
- Submit another member's work under their own identity.

Changes to shared branches must follow the agreed review and merging process. Repository permissions and platform protection settings must be respected.

## 9. Code Quality, Testing, and Definition of Done

All code must be written and maintained to a reasonable professional standard.

Members must aim to produce code that is:

- Readable and understandable.
- Maintainable and consistent with agreed conventions.
- Secure and appropriately documented.
- Compatible with the existing application and database.
- Tested according to the functionality and risk of the change.

Before marking a task as complete, the responsible member must verify that the agreed acceptance criteria have been met, appropriate tests have been performed, and known defects have been reported.

Where applicable, members should confirm that the application builds and runs successfully and that relevant existing functionality remains operational.

Failed tests, known defects, incomplete features, and technical limitations must not be deliberately hidden.

## 10. Security, Privacy, and Responsible Access

All members must protect project data, repository access, database information, credentials, and personal information.

Members must:

- Use authorized accounts and development resources.
- Keep passwords, tokens, API keys, and sensitive configuration values confidential.
- Avoid committing secrets or unnecessary personal data to GitHub.
- Use test data responsibly and protect any real personal information.
- Report suspected security incidents or accidental credential exposure promptly.
- Avoid unauthorized access, modification, deletion, or disclosure of project resources.

If a credential is accidentally exposed, the team must be informed immediately and the credential must be revoked or rotated as appropriate.

## 11. Academic Integrity and Use of AI

All submitted work must comply with the university's academic integrity requirements and assessment instructions.

Members must not:

- Plagiarize code, reports, documentation, or other materials.
- Fabricate test results, project evidence, or contribution records.
- Claim another person's work as their own.
- Falsify GitHub activity or manipulate contribution evidence.
- Use AI-generated content in ways prohibited by assessment rules.
- Present code or explanations that they do not understand as independently completed work when this misrepresents their contribution.

AI tools may be used only in accordance with the applicable assessment rules. Where AI use must be declared or logged, the relevant member must record it accurately.

Members must verify AI-assisted code, understand its purpose, and test it appropriately before including it in the project.

## 12. Transparency, Documentation, and Evidence

The team must maintain accurate and honest records of project activities.

These may include:

- Git commits, branches, and pull requests.
- GitHub issues and project board updates.
- Meeting notes and agreed decisions.
- Test results and defect reports.
- Task assignments and contribution records.
- Releases, documentation, and assessment evidence.

Members must update records when necessary and must not deliberately alter, fabricate, or conceal evidence to misrepresent project progress or individual contributions.

## 13. Constructive Feedback and Code Reviews

Code reviews must be professional, objective, and focused on improving the software.

Reviewers should identify problems clearly, explain the reasons for requested changes, and suggest practical improvements where possible.

Authors must consider reasonable feedback, respond respectfully, and make agreed corrections.

Disagreements about technical decisions should be resolved through evidence, project requirements, testing, and team discussion rather than personal criticism.

Where a disagreement remains unresolved, the Scrum Master should facilitate discussion. Product-related priority decisions should be clarified by the Product Owner, while wider unresolved issues may be referred to the module teaching team.

## 14. Warnings and Corrective Actions

Group 15 will use a fair and proportionate process to address breaches of this Code of Conduct. The purpose is to correct behavior, protect the project, and ensure that all members are treated fairly.

### Level 1: Informal Reminder

An informal reminder may be given for a minor or first-time issue, such as:

- Missing a meeting without notice.
- Forgetting to update a task.
- Submitting work late without communicating the delay.
- Making avoidable mistakes in following the agreed workflow.

The member will be informed of the issue and expected improvement.

### Level 2: Formal Warning

A formal warning may be given when:

- The same problem continues after an informal reminder.
- A member repeatedly misses agreed deadlines without reasonable communication.
- A member repeatedly fails to complete agreed responsibilities.
- Communication or collaboration problems significantly affect the team.
- A member repeatedly ignores agreed review or development procedures.

The warning should record the concern, the relevant evidence, the required corrective action, and an appropriate review date.

### Level 3: Final Warning and Escalation

A final warning and referral to the module teaching team may be appropriate when:

- A member continues the same misconduct after previous warnings.
- Repeated failures significantly threaten project delivery or other members' work.
- A member deliberately misrepresents contributions or project evidence.
- A serious breach of team rules requires formal review.

The member must have a reasonable opportunity to explain the circumstances before conclusions are reached.

### Serious Misconduct

Some conduct may require immediate escalation without following every warning level, including:

- Harassment, threats, or serious discriminatory behavior.
- Deliberate unauthorized access, data destruction, or sabotage.
- Theft or deliberate misrepresentation of another person's work.
- Deliberate fabrication or falsification of academic evidence.
- Intentional disclosure or misuse of confidential information.

Serious concerns must be referred to the appropriate university staff or established reporting process. The team must not impose penalties that exceed its authority.

### Fairness and Record-Keeping

Warnings must be based on identifiable behavior and reasonable evidence, not personal disagreements or unsupported accusations.

Members must be allowed to respond to concerns. Records should be factual, confidential, proportionate, and shared only with relevant people.

Any formal academic or disciplinary decision remains subject to the university's applicable policies and procedures.

## 15. Conflict Resolution and Escalation

When disagreements arise, members should follow these steps:

1. **Direct discussion:** Discuss the problem respectfully with the person concerned.
2. **Team discussion:** If unresolved, arrange a discussion focused on facts, responsibilities, and practical solutions.
3. **Scrum Master involvement:** Ask the Scrum Master to facilitate resolution and clarify the agreed actions.
4. **Product Owner involvement:** Refer product requirements, priorities, and acceptance-criteria disputes to the Product Owner where appropriate.
5. **Teaching team escalation:** If the matter remains unresolved, involves serious misconduct, or affects assessment fairness, seek guidance from the module lecturer or tutor.

Members should not retaliate against anyone who raises a genuine concern in good faith.

## 16. Continuous Improvement

Group 15 will use Sprint Retrospectives and other appropriate discussions to improve teamwork and development practices.

Members will identify what worked well, what needs improvement, and what actions should be taken in the next Sprint.

Agreed improvements should have clear responsibilities and realistic deadlines. This Code of Conduct may be reviewed and updated through team agreement when necessary, provided that changes remain consistent with university requirements.

## 17. Team Commitment and Agreement

By participating in the Group 15 project, all members agree to follow this Code of Conduct, fulfil their responsibilities, respect the agreed workflow, and contribute honestly to the team's shared objectives.

Every member is expected to uphold the principles of respect, equality, honesty, responsibility, accountability, collaboration, professionalism, security, privacy, academic integrity, and continuous improvement.

The team will aim to resolve ordinary problems constructively while ensuring that repeated or serious misconduct is addressed fairly and appropriately.
